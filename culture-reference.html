A five-minute history

Three layers,
one language.

Japanese did not fall from the sky as a single elegant system. It is a pile of layers: old native words, Chinese borrowings, and modern loanwords, all held together by kana and a very patient grammar.

01

Yamato kotoba

やまとことば

The old native vocabulary: 水, 山, 人, 食べる, 美しい. Short, concrete, and often emotional. Most of the words you use in a kitchen or a family are from this layer.

02

Kango

漢語

Words borrowed from Chinese over more than a thousand years: 学校, 経済, 文化, 電話. They carry a formal, written flavour. Most N2 and N1 reading is built from them.

03

Gairaigo

外来語

Modern borrowings, mostly from English: コーヒー, スマホ, アルバイト. They are written in katakana, which is why katakana is your signpost for “this word came from outside”.

  1. c. 300 BCE – 300 CE

    Yayoi Japan

    弥生時代

    Rice farming, bronze and iron, and the first loose political communities. Japan has no writing yet; the language is spoken only.

  2. 710 – 794

    Nara

    奈良時代

    Japan adopts Chinese characters and writes its first chronicles. The Man’yōshū uses characters for their sounds as well as their meanings — the seed of kana.

  3. 794 – 1185

    Heian

    平安時代

    The court at Kyoto produces The Tale of Genji and a refined native vocabulary. Hiragana develops from cursive shorthand; literature becomes a woman’s art as much as a courtier’s.

  4. 1185 – 1603

    Kamakura & Muromachi

    鎌倉・室町

    Warrior government moves power east. Zen Buddhism brings new words and aesthetics; Noh and linked poetry reshape the sound of formal Japanese.

  5. 1603 – 1868

    Edo

    江戸時代

    Two and a half centuries of peace and a booming city culture. Edo (Tokyo) grows into one of the world’s largest cities; its speech begins to overshadow the old Kyoto standard.

  6. 1868 – 1945

    Meiji to war

    明治から戦前

    Modern schools, newspapers and radio push a standard written and spoken Japanese. Thousands of new Chinese-style compounds are coined for science, law and industry.

  7. 1945 – now

    Postwar to today

    戦後から現在

    Broadcast media spreads Tokyo Japanese nationwide, while local dialects survive in homes, comedy and regional pride. English loanwords accelerate with the internet.

Why kana look like that

From picture
to sound.

Early Japanese writers used Chinese characters phonetically, choosing one character per syllable. Cursive handwriting shortened them into flowing hiragana; monks and scholars sliced pieces out of them for katakana.

That is why hiragana look soft and rounded while katakana look angular. They are not two alphabets for two languages — they are two handwriting habits that became two tools.

Eighteen proverbs · ことわざ

Small sentences,
long memories.

Proverbs are the compression algorithm of a culture. Learn one and you get a value, an image and a rhythm in four or five syllables. Read the literal meaning first, then the English idea — the gap between them is where the culture lives.

01ななころび やおき

七転び八起き

nana korobi ya oki

Fall seven times, get up eight.

Keep going. Failure is not the opposite of success; it is the middle of it.

02いしの うえにも さんねん

石の上にも三年

ishi no ue ni mo sannen

Three years on a stone.

Patience wins. Stay long enough and even a cold stone will warm up — a good motto for language study.

03さるも きから おちる

猿も木から落ちる

saru mo ki kara ochiru

Even monkeys fall from trees.

Experts make mistakes too. Say it to comfort someone — or to tease a friend gently.

04はなより だんご

花より団子

hana yori dango

Dumplings over flowers.

Prefer substance to beauty. Said with a smile at cherry-blossom parties, where the snacks are the real attraction.

05でる くいは うたれる

出る杭は打たれる

deru kui wa utareru

The stake that sticks out gets hammered.

Standing out can attract criticism. A warning about group harmony — and a stereotype worth questioning.

06じゅうにん といろ

十人十色

jūnin toiro

Ten people, ten colours.

Everyone is different. A useful counterweight to the “Japan is one homogeneous culture” story.

07あめ ふって じ かたまる

雨降って地固まる

ame futte ji katamaru

After rain, the ground hardens.

A conflict can leave a relationship stronger. Trouble is not always the end of the story.

08ねこに こばん

猫に小判

neko ni koban

Gold coins to a cat.

A valuable thing given to someone who cannot appreciate it. The English cousin is “pearls before swine”.

09いそがば まわれ

急がば回れ

isogaba maware

If you are in a hurry, go around.

The slow, safe route often wins. A perfect proverb for language learners who want to skip the basics.

10いちご いちえ

一期一会

ichigo ichie

One encounter, one lifetime.

Every meeting is unique and unrepeatable. From tea ceremony; useful for travel, friendships and conversations.

11けいぞくは ちからなり

継続は力なり

keizoku wa chikara nari

Continuity is power.

Ten minutes every day beats one heroic weekend. The proverb Japanese teachers repeat when a student wants to finish in a month.

12ちりも つもれば やまとなる

塵も積もれば山となる

chiri mo tsumoreba yama to naru

Even dust, piled up, becomes a mountain.

Small efforts accumulate. One vocabulary card, one sentence, one listening minute — none feels like progress until you look back.

13あんずるより うむが やすし

案ずるより産むが易し

anzuru yori umu ga yasushi

Giving birth is easier than worrying about it.

Doing is often easier than fearing. Use it before a first conversation, a first email, a first trip — the dread is usually heavier than the task.

14なせば なる

為せば成る

naseba naru

If you do it, it will be done.

Attributed to Uesugi Yōzan, an Edo-era lord who revived a struggling domain. The full saying adds: what you cannot do, you simply have not yet made possible.

15しょしん わするべからず

初心忘るべからず

shoshin wasuru bekarazu

Do not forget the beginner’s mind.

From Zeami’s writings on Noh. The beginner’s awkwardness, curiosity and humility are not stages to leave behind; they are the source of improvement.

16あしたは あしたの かぜが ふく

明日は明日の風が吹く

ashita wa ashita no kaze ga fuku

Tomorrow’s wind will blow tomorrow.

Do not carry today’s worry into tomorrow. A gentle way to close a difficult day — and a useful line when a friend is overthinking.

17わらう かどには ふく きたる

笑う門には福来る

warau kado ni wa fuku kitaru

Fortune comes to the house that laughs.

A cheerful household attracts good luck. Said at New Year and in shops; a reminder that mood is part of hospitality.

18せんりの みちも いっぽから

千里の道も一歩から

senri no michi mo ippo kara

Even a thousand-mile road starts with one step.

The best proverb for level one. You do not need to see the whole road; you only need the next step, and then the one after that.

Twelve lines · 名台詞

You already know
the feeling.

Anime and manga are not a separate language. They use the same grammar you meet in lesson one, pushed to an emotional extreme. The last six lines come from Japanese literature, where the same restraint does even more work. Read the line, listen to it, then find the pattern underneath.

“

諦めたらそこで試合終了ですよ。

あきらめたら そこで しあい しゅうりょう ですよ。

If you give up, that is where the match ends.

安西先生 · Slam Dunk

〜たら + そこで: the grammar of a turning point. The sentence is calm, which is why it lands.

“

海賊王に、俺はなる！

かいぞくおうに、おれは なる！

I am going to become the Pirate King!

モンキー・D・ルフィ · One Piece

〜になる in its plain form, with the topic moved forward for drama. Dream sentences are short for a reason.

“

心臓を捧げよ！

しんぞうを ささげよ！

Dedicate your hearts!

エルヴィン・スミス · Attack on Titan

The imperative 〜よ, stripped of politeness. In real life, this tone belongs to speeches, not to a convenience store.

“

生きろ。

いきろ。

Live.

アシタカ · Princess Mononoke

One verb, one imperative. Japanese can carry a whole film in two syllables when the context has already done the work.

“

俺は、お前を超える！

おれは、おまえを こえる！

I am going to surpass you!

ベジータ · Dragon Ball

を marks the person being surpassed; 超える is “go beyond”. Rivalry grammar is surprisingly simple.

“

世界は残酷だ。それでも美しい。

せかいは ざんこくだ。それでも うつくしい。

The world is cruel. And yet, it is beautiful.

ミカサ・アッカーマン · Attack on Titan

Two short sentences, one contrast. それでも is the hinge — the same move you learn for balanced opinions at N2.

“

春はあけぼの。

はるは あけぼの。

In spring, the dawn.

清少納言 · The Pillow Book

The most famous opening in Japanese literature. は sets the topic, and the sentence stops before it explains. Restraint is the style.

“

古池や 蛙飛びこむ 水の音

ふるいけや かわずとびこむ みずのおと

The old pond — a frog jumps in — the sound of water.

松尾芭蕉 · haiku

や is a cutting word: it splits the scene into two images and lets the sound join them. The most famous haiku in the language.

“

やせ蛙 まけるな一茶 これにあり

やせがえる まけるな いっさ これにあり

Lean frog, do not give up — Issa is here.

小林一茶 · haiku

まけるな is a blunt imperative, softened by the poet inserting himself as a cheerleader. Even a frog gets a coach.

“

吾輩は猫である。名前はまだ無い。

わがはいは ねこである。なまえは まだ ない。

I am a cat. As yet I have no name.

夏目漱石 · I Am a Cat

である is the written copula; まだ + negative leaves the name open. The whole novel begins with a grammar joke about status.

“

雨ニモマケズ 風ニモマケズ

あめにも まけず かぜにも まけず

Unbeaten by the rain, unbeaten by the wind.

宮沢賢治 · “Ame ni mo Makezu”

〜にも負けず: “not defeated even by”. The katakana ニ and マケズ are the poem’s original style, not a typo.

“

みんなちがって、みんないい。

みんな ちがって、みんな いい。

Everyone is different, and everyone is good.

金子みすゞ · “Watashi to Kotori to Suzu to”

ちがって is the te-form of ちがう, linking the two halves. A gentle sentence that argues against conformity without raising its voice.

Culture into output

Take one line into your week.

A proverb is only decoration until you use it. This is the smallest useful bridge between the Culture page and the workbook.

  1. Choose one proverb. Copy it by hand, say it three times, then explain it in your own Japanese.
  2. Use it once. Put it in a message to a friend, or in this week’s writing task.
  3. Keep one quote. Write a literary line in your notebook and say why it sticks. That is your first reading journal.
